# Arcs: Collapse and Conflict in the Reach — Comprehensive Rules Reference

## Overview

Arcs is a strategy game by Cole Wehrle (Leder Games) for 2–4 players. Players lead rival spacefaring societies vying for power in a dying interstellar empire called the Reach. The game uses a trick-taking action system where the lead player sets the suit (action type) and other players must follow, pivot, or surpass.

A game of Arcs lasts until someone has enough Power (points). With 4 players, someone needs at least 27 Power. With 3 players, 30 Power. With 2 players, 33 Power. When the game ends, the player with the most Power wins. If no one reaches the threshold, the game ends after Chapter 5 at most.

Players gain Power by completing ambitions — objectives declared during play and scored at the end of each chapter when everyone runs out of action cards. The game typically lasts three to five chapters.

---

## Components

### Player Pieces (per player)
- **Player Board**: Holds resources, unbuilt cities, and Rival pieces taken as Trophies and Captives.
- **Ships (15)**: Move around the map and engage in battles.
- **Cities (5)**: Built on planets, can be taxed to gain resources. Placed in triangle slots on the player board initially; building them opens resource slots.
- **Starports (5)**: Built on planets, used to build ships and enable Catapult moves.
- **Agents (10)**: Placed on cards in the Court to gain influence.
- **Power Markers (2)**: Track current Power on the map edge.

### Cards
- **Action Cards (28)**: Played to take actions and declare ambitions. Suits: Administration, Aggression, Construction, Mobilization. Values 2–6 in all games; values 1 and 7 added for 4-player games only.
- **Guild Cards (25)**: Gained from the Court. Provide new actions, modifiers, and add to ambitions.
- **Vox Cards (6)**: Gained from the Court, resolve immediately with special effects.
- **Setup Cards (12)**: Determine starting positions (4 sets for 2–4 players).
- **Leader Cards (8)**: Give unique powers, starting pieces, and resources (Leaders & Lore mode).
- **Lore Cards (14)**: Give persistent powers (Leaders & Lore mode). No guild icon or raid cost; do not add to ambitions and cannot be stolen.

### Other Pieces
- **Resource Tokens (25)**: 5 each of Material, Fuel, Weapons, Relics, Psionic.
- **Battle Dice (18)**: 6 each of Assault, Skirmish, Raid.
- **Chapter Marker**: Tracks the current chapter.
- **Ambition Markers (3)**: Show which ambitions are declared. Start on blue (lower-numbered) side.
- **Zero Marker**: Covers the card number of the lead card when an ambition is declared, setting it to 0.
- **Initiative Marker**: Shows which player takes the first turn of a round.
- **Path Markers (4)**: Cover gates during setup, making them out of play and connecting neighboring cluster gates.
- **Out-of-Play Markers (6)**: Cover planets during setup, making them out of play.

## The Map

The map consists of 6 clusters. Each cluster has 4 systems: 3 planets and 1 gate (which lists the cluster number).

### Adjacency
A system is adjacent to other systems sharing a thin border.
- Each gate is adjacent to the 3 planets in its cluster and to the gates of its 2 neighboring clusters.
- Each planet is adjacent to the gate in its cluster and to one or both neighboring planets. Planets separated by a thick, irregular border are NOT adjacent.
- The center of the map (with the Arcs logo) is not in play.

### Out-of-Play Clusters
In setup, 1 or 2 clusters are covered with out-of-play markers on their planets and a path marker on their gate. These clusters are out of play in every way. The path marker joins together the gates of the two neighboring clusters, allowing movement between them in a single move. You cannot move into any systems in out-of-play clusters.

### Planets
Each planet has a planet type (Material, Fuel, Weapon, Relic, or Psionic) and 1 or 2 building slots where cities and starports can be built. Each planet also has an ID symbol used in expansions.

## Core Concepts

### Resources and Cities
There are 5 types of resources matching the 5 planet types. You usually gain them by taxing cities and raiding Rivals in battle.

Resources can be spent for Prelude actions and some add to ambitions (Tycoon, Keeper, Empath).

You hold resource tokens in resource slots on your player board. As you build cities (removing them from your board), you open resource slots and uncover Power bonuses for winning ambitions.

When you take a resource, place it in an empty resource slot. You may rearrange resources in your slots, but must discard resources you cannot hold. If a city returns to your board and covers a resource slot, rearrange and discard as needed.

Each resource slot has a raid cost: the number of raid results an attacker must spend to steal it.

### Agents and The Court
Agents are bureaucrats and diplomats. Through play, you place agents on cards in the Court (a row of face-up cards along the map edge). If you have the most agents on a card, you can secure (take) it.

### Damage and Control
Pieces start fresh — ships are fresh when standing upright, buildings are fresh on their fully colored side.

By battling, fresh pieces can become damaged (tipped ships, flipped buildings). If a damaged piece is damaged again, it is destroyed and removed from the map.

**Control**: You control a system and its contents if you have more fresh ships there than each Rival (other player). On a tie, no one controls the system.

Controlling a system lets you tax Rival cities there, capture their agents, restricts how their ships move, and damages pieces they build.

### Trophies and Captives
- **Trophies**: Destroyed Rival pieces go in your Trophies box. Trophies add to the Warlord ambition.
- **Captives**: Captured Rival agents go in your Captives box. Captives add to the Tyrant ambition.

### Key Definitions
- **Loyal**: Of your player color.
- **Rival**: Another player or of their color.
- **Fresh**: Not damaged — an upright ship or a building on its lighter side.
- **Damaged**: Not fresh — a tipped ship or a building on its darker side.
- **Destroy**: Remove a piece from the map (often goes to Trophies).
- **Hit**: Tip over a fresh piece, or remove a damaged piece.
- **Bury**: Place on the bottom of the Court deck.
- **Scrap**: Remove from the game permanently.
- **Capture**: Place the piece in the Captives box on your player board.
- **Steal**: Move a piece from a Rival's play area to yours (distinct from "take" — some rules prevent stealing).
- **Spend**: Return the spent resource to its supply.
- **Adjacent**: Shares a thin border.
- **Building**: Cities and starports.
- **Turn Order**: Starting with the player with initiative and going clockwise.

## Game Structure

### Chapters
A game of Arcs is played over up to 5 chapters. At the end of each chapter, declared ambitions are scored and players gain Power.

### Rounds
Each chapter consists of multiple rounds. In a round, each player takes one turn, starting with the player who has the initiative marker.

### Initiative
The player with the initiative marker leads the first turn of each round. Initiative can change through surpassing or seizing.

---

## Playing a Chapter

### Step 1: Player with Initiative Takes Turn

The player with initiative must play one action card face up as the **lead card**. They may take one action for each action pip on the card based on its suit:
- **Administration** pips can: Tax, Repair, or Influence
- **Aggression** pips can: Battle, Move, or Secure
- **Construction** pips can: Build or Repair
- **Mobilization** pips can: Move or Influence

Place the card in the "Lead" card slot on the left map edge.

#### Passing Initiative
If the player with initiative has no cards or chooses to skip their turn, they must pass the initiative. Give the initiative marker to the next clockwise player who has cards in their hand, then immediately end the round. That player plays the lead card of the new round.

If everyone with cards passes consecutively, discard all action cards and end the chapter.

#### Declaring an Ambition
If you have the initiative, you may declare the ambition shown on your lead card **before taking any actions**:
- "1" cards have no ambition (4 players only).
- "2" through "6" cards let you declare the matching ambition.
- "7" cards let you declare any ambition (4 players only).

Take the highest-number ambition marker from Available Markers and place it in the matching ambition box on the map. Place the zero marker onto the lead card to cover the card number (its number is now 0, but pips are unchanged).

You cannot declare an ambition if all ambition markers are already placed. You cannot declare an ambition multiple times using the same card. You can declare an ambition that was already declared in an earlier round (just add another marker to its box).

### Step 2: Other Players Take Turns

After the lead player, each other player in clockwise order takes a turn. Players with no cards skip their turn.

On your turn, you must play an action card in one of three ways:

#### Surpass
Play an action card face up **of the lead card's suit** with a **higher card number than the lead card** (not every played card — only higher than the lead). You may take actions up to the number of action pips on your card.

Important: Surpassing only requires a card higher than the lead card number, not higher than all played cards.

#### Copy
Play any action card **face down**. You may take **only one action** of the lead card's suit.

#### Pivot
Play an action card face up that is **not** of the lead card's suit. It can have any card number. You may take **only one action** on your card (not the lead card).

Pivoting does NOT change the lead suit.

#### Seizing the Initiative
When you play a card, **before taking any actions**, you can seize the initiative marker from the player who holds it. There are two ways:
1. Play an **extra action card face down** beside the card you played. You do not get actions for it.
2. Surpass with a **"7" action card** (4-player games only). You still get its actions.

Take the initiative marker and lay it down to show it is seized. For the rest of this round, no one else can seize the initiative.

The player who already has initiative cannot seize it. If the initiative is already seized, you can still Surpass with a "7" card — you just won't seize the initiative.

### Step 3: Check Initiative

After everyone has taken a turn:
- If someone **seized** the initiative this round, that player keeps it.
- If no one seized it, give initiative to the player who **Surpassed with the highest card number**.
- If no one Surpassed, initiative stays with its current holder.

### Step 4: Discard Cards and Check for New Round

Discard all played action cards (and any card used to seize initiative) into the action discard pile face down.

If any players have cards left in hand, start the next round. Otherwise, end the chapter.

## Standard Actions in Detail

### Tax (Administration)

Choose a Loyal city, or choose a Rival city you control.

Gain 1 resource of the city's planet type from the supply. Place it in an empty resource slot on your player board.

If you tax a Rival city, you also capture 1 agent of that Rival from their supply (even if you didn't gain the resource).

**Tax Limit**: You cannot tax a city more than once per turn.

**Captives**: Place captured agents in the Captives box on your player board. They add to the Tyrant ambition.

### Build (Construction)

You may build a building or a ship:
- **Building**: Place 1 starport or city in an empty building slot in a system with a Loyal piece. When building a city, use the leftmost city on your player board (this opens resource slots).
- **Ship**: Place 1 ship at a Loyal starport. Each starport can only build one ship per turn.

**Damaged on Build**: When you build anything in a system controlled by anyone other than you, place the piece damaged.

### Move (Mobilization and Aggression)

Move any number of Loyal ships from one system to one adjacent system.

#### Catapult Moves
If you move from a system with a **Loyal starport**, you may take Catapult moves — keep moving the ships as much as you want, dropping them off as you want, until they move to:
- A gate controlled by anyone else, OR
- Any planet

When taking a Catapult move, check for control **before** moving in, not after.

You cannot Catapult from Rival starports you control.

### Repair (Construction and Administration)

Flip up 1 damaged Loyal ship or building anywhere on the map, making it fresh.

### Influence (Mobilization and Administration)

Place 1 agent on any card in the Court row.

### Secure (Aggression)

Take 1 card from the Court if you have more Loyal agents on it than each Rival. Return all Loyal agents on it to your supply. Capture all Rival agents from the secured card (place in Captives box).

Resolve its "When Secured" action, if any. If it says to discard it, place it in the Court discard pile.

Draw a card from the Court deck and place it face up in the Court in the same spot as the secured card.

You cannot immediately use Prelude actions on a card you just secured.

## Battle (Aggression) — Complete Rules

### Step 1: Choose Battle System
Choose 1 system that has any Loyal ships. This is the battle system. Your Loyal ships there are your attacking ships.

### Step 2: Choose Defender
Choose 1 Rival whose pieces are in the battle system. Their pieces there are defending pieces.

### Step 3: Collect Dice
For each attacking ship, you may collect 1 die (assault, skirmish, or raid):
- **Assault dice**: Damage the defender quickly at some cost to you.
- **Skirmish dice**: Damage them slowly but keep you safe.
- **Raid dice**: Steal stuff from them and might damage their buildings, but are risky.

You may collect fewer dice than the maximum. You may mix dice types. You cannot collect more than 6 dice of a type.

**Raid Dice Limit**: You can only collect raid dice if there are defending buildings OR if the defender has no Loyal buildings in any systems on the map.

### Step 4: Roll and Resolve Dice
Roll all collected dice. The **attacker** resolves everything — the defender doesn't usually make choices. Resolve all rolled faces in this order:

1. **Self-hits**: Hit any of your attacking ships once per self-hit result rolled.
2. **Intercept**: If you rolled any intercept results, the defender intercepts you — hit any of your attacking ships once per **fresh defending ship**. This can only happen once per battle regardless of how many intercept results were rolled.
3. **Ship hits**: Hit any of their defending ships once per ship-hit result. Once no defending ships remain, hit any of their defending buildings instead.
4. **Building hits**: Hit any of their defending buildings once per building-hit result.
5. **Raid**: If you have any attacking ships left, you may steal resources and Guild cards by spending raid results (see Raiding below).

### Hitting Pieces and Gaining Trophies
- If you hit a **fresh** piece, it becomes **damaged** (tip a ship, flip a building).
- If you hit a **damaged** piece, it is **destroyed** (remove from map).
- The attacker takes destroyed defending pieces as **Trophies**.
- The defender takes destroyed attacking pieces as **Trophies**.

### Destroying Cities
If you destroy a city — even outside battle — do the following:

1. **Provoke Outrage**: Discard all resources and Guild cards you have of that city's planet type. Place the discarded cards in the Court discard pile. Place an agent to cover the Outrage icon of that city's planet type on your player board — that resource is now Outraged.
   - You cannot spend Outraged resources for their normal Prelude action.
   - You CAN still tax Outraged resources and they still add to ambitions.
   - If you already have Outrage of that type, perform both steps except placing the agent.
   - If you have no Loyal agents, use a placeholder piece until one becomes available.

2. **Ransack the Court**: Secure any card that has any number of the defender's agents. Take all Rival agents on the card as **Trophies**, not Captives.

### Raiding
If any attacking ships remain after taking hits, you may spend raid results to steal resources and Guild cards from the defender, one at a time, by spending the number of raid results shown in the raid cost (on the player board above the resource slot, or in the top-left corner of the Guild card).

## Resources — Prelude Spending

In your turn's Prelude (after playing a card, before spending any action pips), you may spend any number and mix of your resource tokens for their actions:

- **Material**: Take a Build or Repair action.
- **Fuel**: Take a Move action.
- **Weapon**: This turn, you may spend any action pips from your card play to take Battle actions. (Acts like adding "or Battle" to your played card. Weapons cannot turn actions from other resources into Battle actions.)
- **Relic**: Take a Secure action.
- **Psionic**: Take an action listed on the lead card.

Resources spent in Prelude return to the supply at the end of your Prelude, so you cannot regain them until then.

You cannot use Prelude actions on cards you secured from the Court in the same Prelude.

Your Prelude begins once you play an action card and ends once you spend an action pip from your played card.

You cannot spend resources if you pass the initiative or have no cards.

### Cards Holding Resources
Some cards say they hold resources. These resources can be spent and add to ambitions, but they cannot be stolen in battle unless explicitly allowed. If a card holding resources is stolen, the resources stay on it. If it is buried, discarded, or scrapped, resources return to the supply.

---

## Guild Cards

Guild cards have 3 parts:
- **Suit**: Matches 1 of the 5 resources. Material and Fuel cards add to Tycoon. Relic cards add to Keeper. Psionic cards add to Empath. Weapon cards do NOT add to any ambitions.
- **Rules**: Give new actions and modifiers.
- **Raid Cost**: Number of raid results an attacker must spend to steal the card.

### New Actions
Cards can give a new action followed by a standard action name in parentheses, such as "Assassinate (Battle)." When you would take the standard action, you may instead take the new action.

### Modifiers
Cards can give modifiers to standard actions (shown in bold). Modifiers do not modify new actions, but they DO modify standard actions within new actions.

## Ending a Chapter

The chapter ends when everyone runs out of action cards or when everyone with cards passes the initiative.

### Step 1: Score Ambitions

Score each ambition that has any ambition markers in its box on the map:

- **Tycoon**: Have the most total Fuel and Material icons from resources and Guild cards.
- **Tyrant**: Have the most Captives on your player board.
- **Warlord**: Have the most Trophies on your player board.
- **Keeper**: Have the most Relic icons from resources and Guild cards.
- **Empath**: Have the most Psionic icons from resources and Guild cards.

**First place** gains the higher Power shown on ALL its ambition markers.
**Second place** gains the lower Power shown on ALL its ambition markers.

**Ties**: On a tie for first place, all tied players get second place (other players do not place and gain no Power). On a tie for second place, tied players do not place and gain no Power.

**Qualifying**: You cannot gain Power from an ambition if you have none of what it counts.

**Bonus City Power**: Each time you get first place (not tied), gain 2 extra Power if the "+2 to won ambitions" city slot on your player board is uncovered, or gain 5 extra if both "+2" and "+3 to won ambitions" slots are uncovered. You can only gain bonus Power once per ambition regardless of how many markers it has.

#### Two-Player Scoring
During setup with 2 players, 6 resources matching the planets with out-of-play markers are placed on ambition boxes. For scoring, count these resources as if a third player had them. Weapons on the Warlord box count as Trophies. Otherwise, these resources are out of play.

### Step 2: Clean Up and Flip Ambition

If Warlord was scored, return all Trophies. If Tyrant was scored, return all Captives. (Cities refill player board slots from right to left.)

Return all ambition markers to Available Markers on the map.

Flip over the ambition marker with the lowest Power that hasn't been flipped yet to its side with more Power.

### Step 3: End Game or Advance Chapter

The game ends if any player has at least:
- 27 Power (4 players)
- 30 Power (3 players)
- 33 Power (2 players)
- OR if the chapter marker is on Chapter 5.

If the game ends, the player with the most Power wins. On a tie, the tied player earliest in turn order wins.

If the game does not end, advance the chapter marker to the next space.

### Step 4: Draw Cards

Shuffle all action cards. Each player draws 6 action cards.

Discard all action cards not in players' hands into the action discard pile face down, then shuffle it.

The player with initiative starts the next chapter.

**Two-Player Mulligans**: After drawing, the player without initiative may discard their hand of 6 action cards to draw a new hand of 6. They must accept the new hand.

## Setup (Base Game)

### 1. Set Up Table
A. Place the map and collect the 18 battle dice and 25 resource tokens.
B. Give the initiative marker to a random player.
C. Shuffle the 20 action cards marked "2" through "6" to start the action deck.
D. **4 Players Only**: Shuffle the 8 action cards marked "1" and "7" into the action deck.
E. Place the 3 ambition markers on their blue, lower-numbered sides in Available Markers.
F. Place the chapter marker on the "1" space of the Chapter track.
G. Place the zero marker in the Ambition Declared slot on the map.
H. Shuffle all 6 Vox and 25 Guild cards to make the Court deck. Draw 3 cards (2 players) or 4 cards (3–4 players) to make the Court row.
I. Shuffle the setup cards that list your number of players. Draw 1 setup card.
J. In the clusters marked Out of Play on the setup card, place a path marker over the numbered gate and 3 out-of-play markers over the planets touching that gate. (1 cluster out of play for 4 players, 2 clusters for 2–3 players.)
K. **2 Players Only**: Place the 6 resource tokens matching the 6 covered planets onto ambition boxes: Material and Fuel on Tycoon, Weapons on Warlord, Relics on Keeper, Psionics on Empath.

### 2. Set Up Players
L. Each player chooses a color and collects matching board, 5 cities, 5 starports, 15 ships, and 10 agents. Place cities in triangle slots on the player board.
M. Each player puts their Power marker on the "0" space.
N. Players simultaneously place starting pieces: The player with initiative places 3 ships and 1 city in system 1A, 3 ships and 1 starport in system 1B, 2 ships in system(s) 1C. Clockwise from initiative, 2nd/3rd/4th players do the same in 2A–C, 3A–C, 4A–C.
O. Each player gains the 2 resource tokens matching the planet icons in their A and B systems.
P. Each player draws 6 action cards. **2 Players Only**: The player without initiative may discard all 6 and draw 6 new ones.
Q. Discard all remaining action cards face down, then shuffle the discard pile.

## Leaders & Lore Mode

After playing a few base games, players can use Leaders and Lore for unique abilities.

### Setup Changes
Replace Steps N–O of setup with:
1. Lay out lore cards and leader cards face up, each row with cards equal to players plus one.
2. In reverse turn order, each player takes 1 leader or lore card.
3. Continue until each player has 1 leader card and 1 lore card.
4. Each player places starting pieces as shown on their leader card.
5. Each player gains the 2 resource tokens shown on their leader card.
6. If any leaders have extra setup steps, resolve in turn order.

Optional: Let players choose a second lore card for more chaos.

### Leader Cards
Give unique powers, starting pieces, and starting resources.

### Lore Cards
Give persistent powers. They do NOT have a guild icon or raid cost, so they do not add to ambitions and cannot be stolen in battle.

## Fine Print and Technical Rules

### Rules Hierarchy
- This rulebook supersedes aid sheets and player boards.
- Cards supersede the rulebook.
- The term **"cannot"** is absolute — it cannot be superseded unless explicitly named using the term **"ignore"**.
- Italics and parenthetical text are clarifications/reminders, superseded by core rules.

### Negotiations
Players may make deals and promises, but they are NOT binding. Players cannot give each other pieces unless a rule allows it.

### Private Information
Players cannot show the action cards in their hand to anyone else.

### Turn Etiquette
Though declaring an ambition and seizing initiative happen before other actions, players may do them during their turn as long as no new info has been revealed (dice rolled, cards drawn) and relevant modifiers are followed.

### Tiebreakers and Choice Order
If a tie's tiebreaker is not specified, break it in turn order. If players must make decisions in unclear order, resolve in turn order (start with initiative holder, go clockwise).

If multiple things happen simultaneously but must be resolved in order, the player taking their turn chooses the order. If no one is taking a turn, the initiative holder chooses.

### Elimination and Concession
If a player has no starports or ships on the map, they place 3 fresh ships in any gate at the end of their turn.

### Piece Limits
Pieces are limited by box contents except: if you need an agent for an Outrage slot but have none in supply, use a placeholder until one becomes available.

If a rule makes you place pieces, this refers to fresh Loyal pieces by default.

Pieces that you place and return come from/go to their original supply by default, except cities: cities come from/go to the rightmost empty city slot on their player board.

If you must place or return more pieces than possible, do the maximum possible.

If you replace a piece, return it and place another in its place. If the old piece was damaged, the new piece is placed damaged. Replacing does not destroy the piece.

### Split Discard Piles (Optional Tournament Rule)
Normally all action cards are discarded face down. For a more strategic game, split the discard into face-up (played face up) and face-down (played face down) piles. Draw from the face-down pile when prompted.

## FAQ and Common Misunderstandings

**Q: Can I play any suit if I don't have the lead suit?**
A: Yes — you either Pivot (play a different suit face up, get only 1 action of YOUR card's suit) or Copy (play any card face down, get only 1 action of the LEAD card's suit).

**Q: What happens when I Surpass?**
A: You play a card of the lead suit with a higher number than the lead card (not other played cards). You get your full pips. At round end, the highest Surpass takes initiative (unless someone seized it).

**Q: How does seizing the initiative work?**
A: Discard an extra action card face down alongside your played card (getting no actions for it), OR Surpass with a "7" card (4-player only). You must not already have initiative, and no one else can have seized it this round.

**Q: Can ships move through enemy systems?**
A: Yes, but only with Catapult moves. Regular moves go from one system to one adjacent system. Catapult moves continue until reaching a planet or a gate controlled by someone else.

**Q: When can I use Catapult movement?**
A: Only when moving from a system with a Loyal starport. You keep moving, dropping off ships as you want, until you reach any planet or a gate controlled by anyone else. You cannot Catapult from Rival starports you control.

**Q: How does control work exactly?**
A: You control a system if you have more FRESH ships there than each individual Rival. Damaged ships do not count. On a tie, no one controls it.

**Q: When do ambitions score?**
A: At the end of each chapter. All declared ambitions score simultaneously.

**Q: Can I battle in a system without my ships?**
A: No. You need Loyal ships in the system to attack.

**Q: When can I collect raid dice?**
A: Only if there are defending buildings in the battle system OR if the defender has no Loyal buildings anywhere on the map.

**Q: What's the difference between Trophies and Captives?**
A: Trophies are destroyed Rival pieces (from battle). They add to Warlord. Captives are captured Rival agents (from taxing Rival cities or securing cards with Rival agents). They add to Tyrant. Trophies return if Warlord was scored; Captives return if Tyrant was scored.

**Q: Can I declare an ambition that was already declared?**
A: Yes — place another ambition marker in the same box. Both markers' Power values apply to scoring.

**Q: What happens to my card number when I declare an ambition?**
A: The zero marker covers your card number, setting it to 0. Your pips (actions) are NOT affected.

**Q: Can I use Prelude actions from a card I just secured?**
A: No. You cannot use Prelude actions on cards secured from the Court in the same Prelude.

**Q: Does the defender roll dice?**
A: No. The attacker rolls and resolves all dice. The defender's fresh ships cause intercept hits only if the attacker rolled an intercept result.

**Q: What does Outrage do?**
A: It prevents you from spending that resource type for its Prelude action. You can still tax, hold, and count Outraged resources for ambitions.

## Campaign Mode — The Blighted Reach Expansion

The campaign expansion adds a multi-session experience played over three Acts (games). All base game rules apply with additions below.

### Campaign Overview
- A campaign lasts 3 Acts. Acts I and II last 3 chapters each. Act III lasts 4 chapters.
- Do NOT end an Act early if a player gains enough Power. Ignore Power thresholds.
- Do NOT end an Act after 5 chapters. Use the campaign chapter limits instead.
- At the end of Act III, the player with the most Power accumulated over all three Acts wins.
- Tie-break: "A" Fates over "B" Fates, "B" over "C" Fates, then turn order.

### Fates
Each player chooses a Fate card that gives unique objectives and abilities.
- Act I: Choose from "A" Fates.
- After Act I: May switch to a "B" Fate.
- After Act II: May switch to a "C" Fate.

Each Fate has a set of cards and pieces (Fate Set) used during play.

### Objectives
In Acts I and II, your Fate gives you an objective. Place your objective marker on the starting space shown on your objective card. Your objective card gives ways to advance the marker toward 0 Power. When it reaches 0, you complete your objective.

"Winning an ambition" for objectives means getting first place (not tied).

### Grand Ambitions (Act III)
"A" or "B" Fates in Act III get two grand ambitions (gold crosshairs). These are always active — you do not declare them and Rivals cannot gain Power from them.

At the end of a chapter, grand ambitions are scored alongside declared ambitions. Fulfilling one gives the lower Power amount; fulfilling both gives the higher amount.

### Final Objectives (Act III)
"C" Fates in Act III have a final objective. At the end of a chapter, after scoring ambitions, you win the game if your objective marker is at "0" and you have at least 1 Power. This supersedes winning by most Power.

### The Empire

Everyone starts as a **Regent** of a crumbling Empire. One Regent is the **First Regent**, who governs the Empire and holds the Imperial Trust.

A Regent can become an **Outlaw** by calling a Summit. Outlaws can be attacked by the Empire but can battle Regents freely and don't pay into the Imperial Trust.

#### Control and Command
- The Empire controls all systems with any number of fresh Imperial ships, ignoring all players' Loyal ships.
- The First Regent controls all Empire-controlled systems for ambitions, objectives, and events.
- **Presence Law**: On a Regent's turn, they Command Imperial ships in systems where they have Loyal pieces. They control the system if Empire-controlled and can take actions with Imperial ships there (move, repair, battle, build, tax).

#### Movement Law
Regents can only move 1 Imperial ship for each Loyal ship they move along with them at the same time to the same system.

Catapult movement interactions:
- Empire control of gates ends Catapult moves of Outlaw pieces (not Regent or Imperial).
- Regent control of gates ends Catapult moves of Outlaw pieces and Rival Regent pieces alone (not Imperial or mixed Regent/Imperial).
- Outlaw control of gates ends Catapult moves of Regent and Imperial ships alike.

#### Truce Law
Regents cannot harm Imperial ships except for hits they take as attacking ships in battle. Regents cannot harm or tax Rival Regents in systems with Imperial ships.

- When a Regent **attacks**, they MAY add Imperial ships in the battle system as attacking ships.
- When a Regent **defends**, they MUST add Imperial ships in the battle system as defending ships.

#### Outlaw Battles
Outlaws may choose either a Regent or the Empire as defender.
- If the Empire defends, defending pieces are Imperial ships. The First Regent takes destroyed Outlaw pieces as Trophies.
- If there is no First Regent, destroyed Outlaw pieces return to the Outlaw.

---

### Events

There are three kinds of events: Summits, Crises, and Edicts.

Events are triggered by playing an **Event card** or securing the **Imperial Council** from the Court.

#### Event Cards
Event cards are a new kind of action card added during campaign setup. They cannot be scrapped.

On your turn, you may play an Event card. You cannot Lead it, Copy with it, or discard it to seize the initiative.

When you play it, you take actions exactly like the lead card. However, your Event card gives you NO actions if a Rival played another Event card earlier this round or secured the Imperial Council earlier this round.

If you have initiative and only have Event cards, you must pass the initiative.

If you play an Event that gives no actions, you can still take Prelude actions.

**Two-Player Mulligans**: Keep Event cards when mulliganing; only draw the number of non-Event cards you discarded.

#### Resolving Events
At the end of a round where any Event cards were played, after checking initiative:
1. The player with initiative may call the Summit.
2. They roll the event and number dice:
   - Crisis symbol: Resolve Crises.
   - Edict symbol: Resolve Edicts.

If the Imperial Council is secured in the same round as Event cards, resolve the Council and ignore the Event.

---

### The Imperial Council

A card in the Court with two sides: In Session and Decided. Starts In Session each Act.

While In Session, players may influence and secure it. It cannot be buried, scrapped, or discarded.

#### Securing the Council
When you secure it, flip to Decided and tuck under your played card. At round end:
1. Return Imperial Council Decided to the Court.
2. You may call a Summit.
3. If you're a Regent, take the First Regent tile (keeping all Trust resources).
4. If you're an Outlaw, steal resources from the Imperial Trust (up to empty slots). First Regent loses 1 Power per resource stolen.
5. You must resolve either Crises or Edicts.

While Decided, players cannot influence or secure it. It flips to In Session at the start of an Act or when someone Petitions the Council in a Summit.

---

### The Free States

Free buildings (cities and starports) represent independent settlements.

- You may take actions with Free buildings you control — tax Free cities, build ships at Free starports, Catapult from them, and repair them.
- Free buildings can be harmed (battled, hit, damaged, destroyed). Take destroyed Free buildings as Trophies.
- Destroying a Free city still Provokes Outrage.
- Taxing a Free city does NOT let you capture agents.
- If you choose the Free States as defender, all Free buildings in the battle system are defending pieces.
- Raiding Free States: Gain exactly 1 resource matching the planet type by spending raid results. Cannot gain multiple resources from Free States in the same battle.

---

### Summits

Event cards and the Imperial Council let players call the Summit. It has two parts: Call to Order, then Negotiations.

#### Call to Order
The player who called the Summit may take any number of these actions:
- **Return Favors**: Return a Rival's Favors (their agents on your Fate card) to force them to take Negotiation actions you choose. Each forced action costs 1 Favor by default.
- **Petition the Council**: Flip the Imperial Council to In Session.
- **Leave the Empire**: Flip your Regent title to Outlaw. If you're First Regent, give the tile to the Regent with the most Power (resources stay in Trust). If no Regents remain, return all Trust resources (lose 1 Power each) and place the tile in supply.
- **Revive the Empire**: If no Regents exist, flip your Outlaw title to Regent and take the First Regent tile.

#### Negotiations
After Call to Order, all players may take Negotiation actions in a freeform manner. Each requires the consent of the affected Rival:
- **Cede Control**: Replace 1 Loyal piece on the map with 1 Rival piece of that type. Cannot replace a Loyal building if Loyal ships are in its system. Cannot replace your last city, starport, or ship.
- **Delegate Authority**: Replace 1 Loyal agent on a Court card with a Rival's agent.
- **Promise Favor**: Give 1 Loyal agent from supply to a Rival (placed on their Fate card).
- **Transfer Asset**: Give 1 Captive, Trophy, Favor, or resource to a Rival. If it's their Loyal piece, return to their supply. Forcing a resource transfer costs Favors equal to its raid cost.
- **Invite to Empire**: If you're the First Regent, flip an Outlaw's title to Regent.

**Multipart Negotiations**: Players may agree to simultaneously take multiple Negotiation actions.

**Ending Negotiations**: If no one is negotiating, the Summit caller may count down from five seconds to end the Summit.

---

### Crises

When Crises are triggered, resolve in order: Blight Crises, then Fate Crises, then Court Crises.

#### Blight Crises
Each system with Blight has a crisis:
- Each **fresh** Blight deals **3 hits**.
- Each **damaged** Blight deals **1 hit**.

Hits go to Imperial ships first. If none remain, hits go to each player's Loyal ships in the system. Fresh ships take hits before damaged ships.

#### Fate Crises
Some Fates add cards with Crisis text. Resolve in turn order.

#### Court Crises
Roll event and number dice (unless just rolled for an Event card). Resolve Crisis text on each Vox card in the Court from left to right.

**Dice references**:
- Rolled Cluster: The cluster rolled on the number die.
- Planets of the Rolled Symbol: Each planet with the matching ID symbol.
- Rolled Planet: The planet in the rolled cluster with the rolled ID symbol.

If a Crisis removes a card from the Court, return all agents on it and refill the Court.

---

### The Blight

A mysterious entity spreading through the Reach. In Crises it hits ships. In the Intermission it destroys buildings in systems with no ships.

- **Tough**: Takes 2 hits to damage or destroy. One hit does nothing.
- **Battling**: Choose Blight as defender — all Blight in the system are defending pieces. Blight is hit with ship-hit results but NOT building-hit results. Blight does not intercept.
- **Catapult Movement**: Players must end Catapult moves if they enter a gate with any Blight (fresh or damaged).
- **Trophies**: Destroyed Blight are taken as Trophies.
- **Empty Supply**: If you must place Blight but supply is empty, take from the player with the most Blight Trophies.

---

### Edicts

Resolve all Edicts in order from lowest to highest number. The game starts with Govern the Imperial Reach (Edict 03) and Guild Envoys Depart (Edict 00, 2-player only).

#### Govern the Imperial Reach
The First Regent must either **enforce** or **change** the Imperial Policy:

If they **enforce**:
1. **Collect Imperial Demand** — take 1 piece from each Regent based on the Policy:
   - Peace: Relics or Psionics
   - Escalation: Material, Fuel, or Weapons
   - War: Trophies or Captives (not Regent or Imperial pieces)

   Resources go on the First Regent tile (Imperial Trust). The First Regent must also add 1 of the demanded resources from their own supply if they have one.

   If a Rival Regent has none of the demanded pieces, take 1 agent as a Favor. If they have no agents, the First Regent may make them an Outlaw.

2. **Take Imperial Action** based on Policy:
   - Peace — Celebrate Regency: Each Regent gains bonus city Power from uncovered city slots.
   - Escalation — Requisition Ships: Place 2 fresh Imperial ships in an Empire-controlled system (or at a Loyal starport if none).
   - War — Distribute Arms: Give 1 Weapon to each Regent from supply (or Imperial Trust if empty).

If they **change**: Choose a new Govern the Imperial Reach edict card to place on top.

#### Guild Envoys Depart (2-player only)
Bury all Guild cards in the Court with no agents on them, then refill the Court.

---

### Imperial Trust

Resources added to the Imperial Trust on the First Regent tile are **frozen** — they cannot be spent, given, or discarded (even from Outrage), but they still add to ambitions.

Rivals can steal and take frozen resources. Resources on the Trust have no raid cost and cannot be stolen in battle, but Outlaws can steal them by securing the Imperial Council.

Resources return to supply during the Intermission.

---

### Flagships

Some Fates give you a Flagship board, piece, and aid card. When you get a Flagship, replace all Loyal buildings on the map with Free buildings of the same type.

Your Flagship is a fresh Loyal ship that **cannot be removed from the map**.

#### Flagship Rules
- You cannot build Loyal cities or starports on the map. Build them on your Flagship board instead (Upgrades and Armor).
- You can only build up to 1 ship at your Flagship per turn (regardless of starport count on Flagship board).
- In battle, your Flagship collects a variable number of dice and does not deal intercept hits by default.

#### Upgrades and Armor
Your Flagship board has 6 Upgrade slots, each with an Armor slot above it.
- Upgrades give benefits (even when damaged).
- Armor takes hits before its matched Upgrade.
- To build an Upgrade or Armor, your Flagship must be at a planet of the type shown on the slot.
- You can only build Armor if you have its Upgrade and it is fresh.
- When you repair your Flagship, repair an Upgrade or Armor.

Upgrades and Armor do NOT work like normal map buildings:
- Players cannot tax these cities or use these starports to build ships or Catapult.
- Destroying these cities does NOT Provoke Outrage or Ransack the Court.

#### Flagship Upgrade Benefits
- **Ship Crane** (Material/Fuel): Build up to 2 ships per turn in Flagship's system (still requires 2 build actions).
- **Defense Array** (Weapon/Psionic): When defending, Flagship deals intercept hits equal to fresh starports on Flagship board.
- **Slipstream Drive** (Fuel/Relic): Once per turn in Prelude, Catapult move Flagship alone or other ships from Flagship.
- **Control Array** (Psionic/Material): While Flagship is in a gate, tax any cities in its cluster like Loyal (don't take Captives).
- **Tractor Beam** (Relic/Weapon): When Flagship defends, attacker cannot collect raid dice.
- **Hull** (Any/Any): No unique benefit, but cities open resource slots and starports add battle dice.

#### Flagship Battles
**Attacking**: Flagship collects dice equal to fresh starports on Upgrades/Armor. While attacking, Upgrades and Armor are hit like ships.

**Defending**: Rivals can collect raid dice if there are defending buildings or a defending Flagship. Flagship does NOT intercept unless it has Defense Array. While defending, Upgrades and Armor are hit like buildings. Armor is always hit before its Upgrade.

#### Flagships in Events
In Crises, Flagship only takes hits if no other fresh Loyal ships remain. Take hits with rightmost Armor first, then rightmost Upgrade.

In Summits, cannot use Cede Control to replace Loyal buildings on Flagship board.

---

### Intermission

Happens at the end of Acts I and II.

**A. Resolve Fate**: Each player resolves their Resolution card. Failed objectives lose Power equal to the objective marker's position on the Power track.

**B. Clear Court**: Return all Court cards except Imperial Council to the deck. Return all agents. Scrap the Court discard pile.

**C. Clear Pieces**: Return all Trophies and Captives. Return all frozen resources (including Imperial Trust). 2-player: Return ambition box resources.

**D. Repair and Destroy Pieces**:
1. Repair all damaged Blight.
2. Destroy all damaged ships, buildings, and Flagship Upgrades/Armor.
3. Destroy all buildings in systems with Blight and no ships.

**E. Shift Initiative and Power**:
1. Give initiative to the player with the most Power.
2. Each player with more than 1 Power loses half (round to lose less).

**F. Choose Fate**:
- Completed objective: Draw 1 Fate card.
- Failed objective: Return current Fate to box, draw 2 Fate cards.
- Draw "B" Fates after Act I, "C" Fates after Act II.
- Secretly choose 1, reveal simultaneously.
- If choosing a new Fate: return old Fate card, scrap unused Fate Set pieces not in play, return all Favors to Rivals, take new Fate Set.

---

### Campaign New Rules and Terms

**Protected Guild Cards**: Shown by "X" instead of raid cost. Cannot be removed from holder's play area unless they Provoke Outrage of its suit.

**Attaching Cards**: Place above a Court card without an attached card. When you secure a card, also secure its attached card. If a card is removed from the Court and has an attached card, the attached card slides down and takes all agents from the removed card.

**Frozen Resources**: Cannot be spent, given, or discarded (even from Outrage), but CAN be stolen/taken by Rivals (including Imperial Demand). Still add to ambitions.

**Harm**: Choosing as defender in battle, damaging, destroying, or hitting.

**Tokens (Fate)**: By default, tokens added by Fates cannot be harmed and are not Loyal. Some override these defaults.

---

### Campaign Setup — Act I

Key differences from base game setup:
- No setup cards. No clusters out of play.
- Use campaign Guild and Vox cards (marked "CC"), not base Court cards (marked "BC").
- Use the same lore deck (marked "L"), shuffled into the Court deck after dealing.
- Add 2 Event cards (2–3 players) or 3 Event cards (4 players) to the action deck.
- Set up Imperial clusters: Roll number die, place 1 Imperial ship in each system of that cluster and the next clockwise cluster (8 total).
- Roll event die. Place 1 Free city on each planet of the rolled symbol outside Imperial clusters.
- Place 1 damaged Blight in each system outside Imperial clusters.
- Players draw 2 "A" Fate cards and secretly choose one.
- Players set up based on their Fate's Setup card, then flip to Objective side.
- Players place starting pieces in turn order at planets with Imperial ships.
- Place Free cities in all empty building slots at planets with Imperial ships.

### Campaign Setup — Acts II and III

Same as previous Act with these changes:
- Flip lowest-Power ambition marker(s) to higher-Power side (1 in Act II, 2 in Act III).
- Shuffle Court deck and deal fresh Court. Flip Imperial Council to In Session.
- Place 1 damaged Blight on each gate with no Blight and no ships.
- Roll event die and place additional Blight based on results.
- Each player sets up using their new Fate's Setup card.
- Place chapter track tile on current Act's side.
