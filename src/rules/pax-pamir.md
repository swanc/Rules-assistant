# Pax Pamir 2nd Edition — Rules Reference

## Overview

Pax Pamir 2nd Edition is a game by Cole Wehrle for 1–5 players set during the Great Game in 19th-century Afghanistan. Players are Afghan political leaders navigating the power struggle between three foreign coalitions (British, Russian, and Afghan) while building their own courts of cards. The game uniquely combines tableau building, area control, and shifting loyalties.

---

## Components

- 1 Map of Afghanistan with 6 regions connected by borders
- Court cards (political, economic, military, and intelligence cards)
- Coalition blocks: British (red), Russian (yellow), Afghan (black)
- Player cylinders (tribes, roads, spies)
- Rupees (currency)
- Favored suit marker
- Dominance check cards (4 in the deck)
- Market cards (6 face-up in the market)

---

## Core Concepts

### Coalitions
There are 3 coalitions:
- **British** (red blocks)
- **Russian** (yellow blocks)
- **Afghan** (black blocks)

Each player is **loyal** to exactly one coalition at any time. Your loyalty is shown by the coalition symbol on your leftmost eligible court card. You can change loyalty by purchasing cards.

### Loyalty
Your loyalty determines:
- Which coalition's dominance you benefit from during Dominance Checks.
- Which blocks count as "your" pieces for influence and control.
- Your score is determined by how well your coalition performs AND your personal influence.

### The Court
Each player has a personal **court** — a row of face-up cards in front of them. Court cards provide:
- **Actions** (stars): Each card has 0–2 action icons.
- **Loyalty**: Your leftmost card with a coalition symbol sets your loyalty.
- **Special abilities**: Ongoing or triggered effects.
- **Regional influence**: Many cards grant presence in specific regions.

---

## Game Structure

### The Market
A row of 6 face-up cards. These are the cards available for purchase. The market is divided into two rows:
- **Row 1** (slots 1–2): Cost 0 rupees each
- **Row 2** (slots 3–4): Cost 2 rupees each
- **Row 3** (slots 5–6): Cost 4 rupees each

When a card is purchased or discarded, remaining cards slide down (left), and a new card is drawn to fill the rightmost slot.

### Turns
On your turn, you take **2 actions** (can be the same action twice):
1. **Purchase** a card from the market.
2. **Play** a card from your hand to your court.
3. Use a **card action** from a card in your court.

### Hand Limit
You can hold up to **2 cards** in your hand. If you exceed this, discard down to 2.

### Card Actions
Cards in your court have action icons (stars). When you use a card action, you choose one of that card's available action types. Each card can be used once per turn (rotate it to show it's been used; unrotate at end of turn).

---

## Actions in Detail

### Purchase
- Pay rupees equal to the card's market position cost (0, 2, or 4).
- Take the card into your hand.
- If the card shows a different coalition than your current loyalty, **your loyalty may change** when you later play it (if it becomes your leftmost coalition card).

### Play a Card
- Play a card from your hand to the rightmost position in your court.
- If the card has a "play effect" (immediate ability), resolve it now.
- Check if your loyalty has changed (leftmost coalition card).

### Card Actions (Starred Actions)
Each card may have 1–2 action icons. When you use a card action, choose one of these types:

#### Tax
- Take rupees from the bank equal to the number of **your loyalty's blocks** (armies + roads) on the map, plus any tax bonuses.
- Alternatively, take rupees equal to the number of market cards showing your loyalty's coalition.

#### Build
- Place a coalition block (army or road) corresponding to a specific region listed on the card.
- **Armies**: Go into a region on the map.
- **Roads**: Go onto a border between two regions.

#### Move
- Move 1 **army** from one region to an adjacent region (connected by a border).
- OR move 1 **spy** (your cylinder) from one card to an adjacent card in any player's court.

#### Betray
- Remove 1 **coalition block** from a region where you have a **spy** on a card associated with that region.
- OR remove 1 **road** from a border adjacent to a region where you have influence.
- Costs: Betray is free if the target is not your own coalition. If targeting your own coalition, it costs rupees.

#### Gift
- Give rupees to another player to gain their favor or for diplomatic purposes.
- Mechanical benefit: Giving 2+ rupees to another player lets you place a **gift token** on their court.

#### Spy
- Place one of your cylinders as a **spy** on a card in any player's court (including your own).
- Spies provide access to that card's region for Betray actions and protect your cards.

## Tribes, Roads, and Spies

### Tribes (Cylinders on Map)
Your cylinders placed on the map represent **tribes**. Tribes give you influence in a region and count during Dominance Checks.

### Roads (Cylinders on Borders)
Your cylinders placed on borders are **roads**. Roads connect regions and provide economic/logistic benefits.

### Spies (Cylinders on Cards)
Your cylinders placed on court cards are **spies**. Spies:
- Protect the card they're on (harder to remove).
- Enable Betray actions in the card's region.
- Count as influence for Dominance Checks via the card's region.

---

## Dominance Checks

**Dominance Checks** are the scoring events in Pax Pamir. There are 4 Dominance Check cards shuffled into the deck. When one is revealed from the market deck, a Dominance Check occurs.

### How Dominance Checks Work

1. **Determine Dominance**: Check if any single coalition **dominates**. A coalition dominates if it has **more blocks on the map** than each other coalition **individually** by a margin of at least 4 (in a 4–5 player game) or some threshold based on player count.

2. **If a coalition dominates**:
   - Players loyal to the dominant coalition **score points** based on their **influence**.
   - Influence = number of your cylinders (tribes, roads, spies on cards with the dominant coalition's symbol) associated with that coalition + number of court cards matching the dominant coalition.
   - The player with the most influence in the dominant coalition scores the most points.

3. **If no coalition dominates** (no coalition has enough margin):
   - **Loyalty doesn't matter**. Instead, all players score based on the total number of **cylinders they have on the map** (tribes + roads).
   - This rewards players who spread their physical presence.

4. After scoring, a **cleanup phase** occurs:
   - Remove blocks from the map (partially — the dominant coalition loses some blocks).
   - The favored suit may change.
   - The game continues until the 4th Dominance Check or the deck runs out.

### Influence in Detail
Your influence is specific to a coalition:
- **Tribes** you have in regions with that coalition's armies.
- **Spies** on cards associated with that coalition.
- **Court cards** you own that show that coalition's symbol.
- **Roads** on borders adjacent to that coalition's regions.

---

## The Favored Suit

One of the four card suits (Political, Intelligence, Economic, Military) is the **favored suit** at any time, tracked by a marker. The favored suit provides bonuses:
- Cards of the favored suit in the market are discounted.
- Some abilities trigger only when their suit is favored.
- The favored suit can change during Dominance Checks.

---

## Rupees

Rupees are the currency. You start with a set amount and earn more through:
- **Tax** action.
- **Card effects** that generate income.
- **Gifts** from other players.

Rupees are spent on:
- **Purchasing** cards from the market.
- **Playing** some cards (those with a cost).
- Certain **actions** that have a rupee cost.

**Important**: Rupees on cards in the market belong to no one. When you buy a card, any rupees on it go to the previous owner (or bank).

---

## Winning

The game ends after the **4th Dominance Check** or when the deck runs out. The player with the most **victory points** accumulated from Dominance Checks wins.

Tiebreaker: The player with the most rupees wins ties. If still tied, the player with the most court cards wins.

---

## Key Timing Rules

- **Loyalty changes**: Immediately when your leftmost coalition court card changes (via playing or losing cards).
- **Dominance Checks**: Trigger immediately when a Dominance Check card is revealed from the deck.
- **Card effects**: "When played" effects resolve immediately. "While in court" effects are ongoing.
- **Spy placement**: Can go on any player's court card, not just your own.
- **Betrayal**: Can only target blocks in regions where you have a spy or influence via court cards.

---

## Setup

1. Place the map board.
2. Set starting coalition blocks on the map according to the setup guide.
3. Shuffle the court deck (including 4 Dominance Check cards).
4. Deal the market: place 6 face-up cards in the market row.
5. Give each player their starting rupees (4 rupees each) and cylinders.
6. Each player starts with **no court cards** and **no loyalty** (neutral until they play/purchase a coalition card).
7. Determine start player (youngest player, or random).
8. Begin play clockwise.

---

## Player Count Adjustments

- **1 player**: Solo mode with an AI opponent (Wakhan).
- **2 players**: Modified Dominance Check thresholds. More tactical, less diplomatic.
- **3 players**: Standard rules. Good balance of diplomacy and strategy.
- **4–5 players**: Full diplomacy. Dominance is harder to achieve, making "no dominance" scoring more common.

## FAQ / Common Misunderstandings

**Q: How does loyalty change?**
A: Your loyalty is always determined by the **leftmost card** in your court that shows a coalition symbol. If you play, purchase, or lose cards that change which is leftmost, your loyalty changes immediately.

**Q: Can I be loyal to no coalition?**
A: Yes. If you have no court cards with coalition symbols, you are **neutral**. During a Dominance Check, neutral players only score in the "no dominance" scenario.

**Q: Do spies count for Dominance?**
A: Spies count for your **influence** in a coalition (affecting how many points you score), but they do not count as coalition blocks on the map (which determine whether a coalition dominates).

**Q: What happens to rupees on market cards?**
A: When you purchase a market card, any rupees sitting on it go to you as a bonus. When cards slide in the market, rupees stay on their cards.

**Q: Can I betray my own coalition?**
A: Yes, but it typically costs more rupees. This can be strategically useful to prevent your coalition from becoming too dominant if you're not in a strong scoring position.

**Q: How many actions do I get per turn?**
A: Exactly 2 actions. Each action can be: purchase, play, or use a card action. You can repeat the same action type.

**Q: When does the game end?**
A: After the 4th Dominance Check card is resolved, or when the deck is exhausted (whichever comes first). If the deck runs out, a final Dominance Check occurs.
