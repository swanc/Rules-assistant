# Pax Penning — Rules Reference

## Overview

Pax Penning is a strategic game for 3–5 players set in Viking Age Sweden (995–1030 AD). Players are chieftains and landowners navigating the transition from the old Viking society to a new feudal kingdom under Olof Skötkonung, the first Christian King of Sweden. The game centres on the town of Sigtuna and the surrounding countryside.

**Key idea:** You never spend coins — they represent allegiance. The Penning side means loyalty to the King; the Silver side gives you more dice. The tension between these two sides drives the whole game.

**Win condition:** The game can end in two different ways (Thegn's Victory or Jarl's Victory), and each has different scoring. There's also an Heir mechanic where someone can steal your victory.

---

## Key Concepts

### Coins (Pennings and Silver)
Each coin has two sides:
- **Penning side** (King's face) — Determines your market reach (which chess pieces you can access). More Pennings = access to more powerful actions.
- **Silver side** (blank) — Gives you bonus dice to roll at the start of your turn. More Silver = more dice = more options.

You never spend coins. They stay in front of your screen the whole game. Other effects can flip them.

### Stones
Glass stones are your main resource. They move between locations:
- **Your House** (behind your screen) — your private supply
- **Sigtuna** (on the board) — a shared area; you can pull stones from here for dice
- **Other players' Houses** — hidden stones placed there give you potential Heir claims
- **Crossroads** (as Runestones) — placed on the map, they earn you stones when pawns pass them

### The Hierarchy
A column on the board ranking all players. It determines:
- Turn order for voting at the Thing
- Tiebreakers for victory
- Who wins in a Jarl's Victory

You can swap adjacent positions in the Hierarchy by spending pairs during the Feast phase.

### Chess Pieces
The game uses 8 chess pieces (King/Queen, Knight, Bishop, Rook, 4 Pawns) as action pieces. They sit in market bowls and cycle through them. Each piece triggers a different action when you use it.

### Market Bowls
Bowls numbered by cost (in Pennings). Chess pieces move between bowls — they start at the top (most expensive) and drift down each turn. You can only pick from bowls whose value doesn't exceed your Penning count.

## Setup

1. Place the King/Queen in Sigtuna and the 3 matching pawns on the lower 3 starting stars
2. Place Knight, Bishop, Rook, and last Pawn randomly in the top 4 market bowls (1 per bowl)
3. Each player takes: a screen, all stones of their colour, and 2 coins (random side up). Stones go behind your screen (your House), coins go in front
4. Fill the Hierarchy with 1 stone per player in random order. Place 1 coin at the bottom (Silver side up)
5. Last player in Hierarchy rolls 1 die and places it on an empty Road space as a Roadblock
6. In Hierarchy order, each player places:
   - 4 stones in Sigtuna
   - 2 stones in every other player's House
   - 1 stone at an empty Crossroads (as a Runestone)
   - 1 die on an empty Road space
7. Start play from the top of the Hierarchy, then clockwise

**3-player adjustment:** Start with 1 extra coin. Place 2 more stones in other Houses and 1 more Runestone.

---

## On Your Turn

Each turn has 4 steps:

### 1. Gathering — Roll the Dice

Roll dice equal to:
- 1 die per **Silver** coin you have
- Plus 1 die per **stone you pull from Sigtuna** to your House (you can also take stones from another player's House with their permission)
- Minimum 1 die even if you have nothing

**Important:** The King blocks the coin he's standing on — it gives no bonus dice and doesn't count toward your market reach.

### 2. Feast — Check for Pairs

For each pair you rolled, move 1 stone from your House to any player's House, then do one of:
- **Change Standing:** Swap 2 adjacent pieces in the Hierarchy
- **Announce Loyalty:** Flip any player's coin to its other side

3-of-a-kind = 1 pair. 4-of-a-kind = 2 pairs.

When sending stones to another player's House, keep them secret (they go behind that player's screen).

### 3. Leverage Two Royal Connections

Choose 2 market bowls (you can't pick bowls costing more Pennings than you have). Take the chess piece from each bowl, assign one of your rolled dice to each, and resolve the actions.

If a bowl is empty, use the printed piece instead (King or Pawn). You may pick both zero-cost bowls. If you only have 1 die, choose only 1 connection.

### 4. Clean Up

- Move all chess pieces 1 bowl down (they gather in the zero-cost King bowl)
- Place the pieces you just used in the top bowl
- Return remaining dice to the supply

## Royal Connections (Chess Piece Actions)

X = the value of the die you assigned. You must use the full value unless blocked.

### ♚ King or Queen
1. Place a stone from your House on an empty Crossroads as a Runestone
2. Move the King X steps clockwise along the player coins (the King's Path)

You can only use the King action once per turn. The King's Path goes clockwise from Sigtuna through all player coins and back. When the King returns to Sigtuna, **the Thing is held** at the end of that turn.

### ♜ Rook
Place the assigned die on an empty Road space (as a Roadblock).

**Critical:** If you place the last available die, the game immediately ends and **everyone loses**.

### ♞ Knight
Remove a die with value X or less from a Road space (clears a Roadblock).

### ♝ Bishop
Move X stones from your House to another player's House.

### ♟ Pawn
Move a pawn X steps along Road spaces toward Sigtuna.
- Difficult Road spaces cost 2 steps
- Dice on roads cannot be passed (they block movement)
- When a pawn passes a Runestone, its owner moves 1 stone from their House to Sigtuna
- If the pawn reaches Sigtuna or lands on another pawn: place 1 stone in Sigtuna, return the pawn to any starting star, and stop

---

## Holding the Thing

The Thing happens when the King returns to Sigtuna (at the end of that player's turn).

### 1. Vote on Supporting Royalty
In Hierarchy order, each player chooses whether to support the King. Those who support roll 1 die each.

If the **highest die rolled ≥ number of dice on roads**, flip the Hierarchy Coin to Penning side.

If the Hierarchy Coin is **already on the Penning side**, the game ends immediately in **Thegn's Victory**.

### 2. Welcome the Vikings Home
Each player takes 1 coin from the supply and places it with a random side up.

If this is the **third Thing**, the game ends:
- **Thegn's Victory** if the Hierarchy Coin is on the Penning side
- **Jarl's Victory** otherwise

---

## End of Game — Who Wins?

### Thegn's Victory (King succeeds)
The player with the **most Pennings** wins. Ties broken by Hierarchy order.

### Jarl's Victory (King fails)
The player **highest in Hierarchy order** wins — but **excluding** whoever has the most Pennings. If there's a tie for most Pennings, all tied players are excluded.

### Heir's Victory (stealing the win)
After determining the winner, check: does any player have the **most stones in the winner's House**? If so, that player steals the victory as the winner's Heir.

This can chain — if someone then has the most stones in the Heir's House, they steal it again. If there's a complete circle of Heirs, those players form a **Coalition** and all win together.

You break ties in your own House however you wish.

### Everyone Loses
If the last available die is placed on a Road (via the Rook action), the game ends immediately and nobody wins. The roads are completely blocked and society collapses.

---

## Strategy Notes

- **Silver vs Penning tension:** Silver gives you more dice (more options), but Pennings give you access to better chess pieces and win you the Thegn's Victory. You can't have both.
- **Stones in other Houses:** Hidden stones placed in opponents' Houses set up Heir claims. The winner might not stay the winner.
- **Roadblocks matter:** Dice on roads block pawn movement AND make the Thing vote harder to pass (you need to roll higher). The Knight clears roads; the Rook blocks them.
- **The Hierarchy is fluid:** Pairs let you swap positions, which matters for Jarl's Victory, voting order, and tiebreakers.
- **The King's position:** Wherever the King stands, that player's coin is blocked. Timing the King's movement is important.
