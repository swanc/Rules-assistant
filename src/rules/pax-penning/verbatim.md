# Pax Penning — Verbatim Rulebook

Source: Official rulebook by Matilda Simonsson / Milda Matilda Games (2023)

---

## Introduction

Pax Penning takes place between 995 and circa 1030 AD, in the transition from the Viking Age to the Medieval Age, focusing on the small eastern town of Sigtuna and its surrounding countryside. Olof Skötkonung, the first Christian King of Sweden, is trying to strengthen his hold on this region, while simultaneously importing political concepts from Europe to compensate for his own weaknesses. The players determine how Sigtuna reacts to this new power-play— co-operation or condemnation.

This game is also about lost history; it takes place in the transition between archaeological evidence and written record. Currency and runestones can tell us much, but there's even more we'll never know about. The game conveys things I'm fairly certain are true, but the people who knew are long gone. Our sources are forever incomplete, and the picture the game paints is the same.

History is seldom simple, and it's important to remember that blanks are blanks. Don't let fascists and white nationalists fill them in.

— Matilda Simonsson, 2023

## Setup

1. Unfold the map. Place the King/Queen in Sigtuna, and the 3 matching pawns on the lower 3 starting stars.
2. Place the Knight, Bishop, Rook, and last Pawn randomly in the top 4 market bowls (1 per bowl).
3. Each player seats themselves around the board and takes the following: a screen, all stones in the matching color and 2 coins. Place the stones behind your screen and your coins in front of your screen with a random side up. This is your House.
4. Fill the Hierarchy with 1 stone from each player in a random order. Place 1 coin at the bottom with the Silver side up.
5. The last player in the Hierarchy order rolls 1 die and places it on an empty Road Space as a Roadblock.
6. In Hierarchy Order (top to bottom), each player places 4 stones in Sigtuna, 2 stones in every other player's House, 1 stone at an empty Crossroads as a Runestone, and rolls and places 1 die on an empty Road.
7. Begin play, starting with the player at the top of the Hierarchy Order. Then continue Clockwise.

At 3 players: Start with 1 more coin. Place 2 more stones in the other players' Houses and 1 more stone at an empty Crossroads.

## On Your Turn

### 1. Gathering — Roll the Dice

Roll 1 die per Silver you have plus 1 die per stone you choose to move from Sigtuna to your House. With another player's blessing, you may move their stones to your House. If you cannot get any dice, you still roll 1 die.

### 2. Feast — Check for Pairs

For each set of pairs rolled, move 1 stone from your House to another player's House and choose one:

- ❖ Change Standing: Swap 2 adjacent pieces in the Hierarchy.
- ❖ Announce Loyalty: Flip any player coin to its other side.

3 of a kind counts as 1 pair. 4 of a kind counts as 2 pairs.

You can always place and give stones of any color from your House. When sending stones to another House, keep them secret!

### 3. Leverage Two Royal Connections

Choose 2 market bowls. You cannot choose market bowls whose value exceeds the amount of Pennings you have. Take 1 chess piece from each, assign them 1 die you rolled, and resolve them (if you only have 1 die, choose 1).

If a bowl is empty, resolve the printed chess piece instead (King or Pawn). You may choose both zero-cost bowls.

### 4. Clean Up

Move all chess pieces 1 bowl down (they gather in the zero value King bowl), then place the chess pieces you used in the top bowl. Return remaining dice to the general supply.

## Resolving Royal Connections I

X refers to the value of the assigned die. You must resolve the full value unless a piece cannot walk any further or you have no more stones in your House.

### ♚ King or Queen

Place a stone from your House on an empty Crossroads as a Runestone, then move the King X player coins clockwise. You can only take the King's action once per turn. Additionally, the coin that the King is standing on is blocked (it gives no bonus dice nor does it increase your market reach).

The King's Path starts in Sigtuna and continues clockwise along the player coins (starting with the player sitting to the left of Sigtuna). Each coin takes 1 step and when the King has walked all the way around he returns to Sigtuna to Hold the Thing (page 7). The King stops when he reaches Sigtuna, regardless of how many steps he has left.

### ♜ Rook

Place the die on an empty Road space. If you place the last die, the game ends and everyone loses.

## Resolving Royal Connections II

X refers to the value of the assigned die. You must resolve the full value unless a piece cannot walk any further or you have no more stones in your House.

### ♞ Knight

Remove a die with value X or less from a Road space.

### ♝ Bishop

Move X stones from your House to another player's House.

### ♟ Pawn

Move a pawn X Road spaces towards Sigtuna. Difficult Road spaces count as 2 steps and dice cannot be passed. For each runestone you pass, its owner places 1 stone from their House in Sigtuna.

If the pawn reaches Sigtuna or lands on another pawn, place 1 stone in Sigtuna, return the pawn to a starting star of your choice and stop moving.

## Holding The Thing

When the King returns to Sigtuna, hold the Thing at the end of the player's turn.

### 1. Vote On Supporting Royalty

In Hierarchy order, each player decides if they want to support Olof Skötkonung. If they do, they roll 1 die. If the highest rolled die is equal to or greater than the number of dice on roads, flip the Hierarchy Coin to the Penning side. If it's already on the Penning side, the game immediately ends in the Thegn's Victory.

### 2. Welcome the Vikings Home

Each player takes 1 coin from the supply and places it in front of their screen with a random side up.

If this is the third Thing, the game ends in the Thegn's Victory if the Coin is at the top of the Hierarchy, or the Jarl's Victory otherwise.

## The End of the Game

### Thegn's Victory

The player with the most Pennings wins. Ties are broken in Hierarchy Order. If the Thegn is in a Coalition, the entire Coalition wins.

### Jarl's Victory

The player highest in Hierarchy Order wins, excluding the player(s) with the most Pennings. If the Jarl is in a Coalition, the player with the most stones in their House wins.

### Heir's Victory and Coalitions

If you have the most stones in the winner's House, you steal their victory as their Heir. If another player then has the most stones in your House, they become your Heir and steal your victory (and so on).

If there's a complete circle of Heirs, those players form a Coalition.

You break ties in your House as you wish.
