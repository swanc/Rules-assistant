# Pax Pamir 2nd Edition — Comprehensive Rules Reference

## Overview

Pax Pamir 2nd Edition is a tableau-building game for 1–5 players. Players are nineteenth-century Afghan leaders navigating the power struggle between three coalitions (British, Russian, Afghan) after the collapse of the Durrani Empire. Players purchase cards from a central market, play them into a personal court, and use card-based actions to influence the map, disrupt opponents, and score victory points during Dominance Checks.

---

## Components

- **Map**: 6 regions (Kabul, Kandahar, Herat, Punjab, Transcaspia, Persia) connected by borders. No limit on pieces per region or border.
- **Coalition blocks (36)**: 12 each — British (pink/red), Russian (yellow), Afghan (green/black). Blocks placed in regions are **armies**; blocks placed on borders are **roads** (set on their side).
- **Player cylinders (11 per player)**: 1 for VP track, 10 for placement. Cylinders in regions are **tribes**; on court cards are **spies**; on the loyalty dial are **gifts**.
- **Rupees (36 coins)**: Each worth 1 rupee. Represent political capital. Finite but rarely exhausted.
- **Court cards (100)**: The main cards players purchase and play.
- **Event cards (16)**: 4 are Dominance Checks; 12 are other events with top (discard) and bottom (purchase) effects.
- **Wakhan AI cards (24 + 2 aid)**: Used only for solo/2-player with automated opponent.
- **Ruler tokens (6)**: One per region, held by the player who rules that region.
- **Favored suit marker**: Tracks which of the 4 suits is currently favored.
- **Loyalty dials and player boards**: One per player. Loyalty dial shows current coalition; player board stores unused cylinders and shows privilege tracks.

---

## The Four Suits

Each court card belongs to one of four suits:

- **Political (purple hat)**: Consolidates power and controls which cards can be played. Total purple stars in your court increase your **court size limit** (base 3 + purple stars).
- **Intelligence (blue eye)**: Grants diplomatic flexibility and ability to compromise enemies. Total blue stars increase your **hand size limit** (base 2 + blue stars).
- **Economic (yellow wheat)**: Controls rupee flow, movement, and protects wealth. Total yellow/gold stars provide your **tax shelter** — rupees up to this amount cannot be taxed from you.
- **Military (red swords)**: Commands armies and secures coalition dominance. Total red stars serve as the **final tiebreaker** at game end.

---

## Card Anatomy

### Core Features (all court cards)

- **Region**: The region the card is associated with (top of card).
- **Suit and Rank**: Rank is 1–3 stars. Rank determines action strength and contributes to suit privilege totals.
- **Card-based actions**: 0–2 action icons on the card. Each card can only be used for ONE action ONCE per turn.
- **Impact icons**: Resolved from top to bottom when the card is played. These place pieces on the map or grant immediate effects.

### Advanced Features (some court cards)

- **Special ability**: A text box with an ongoing or triggered effect. Active as long as the card is in your court. Special abilities are NEVER held hostage.
- **Patriot (colored bar)**: Patriots have a coalition loyalty. They will only serve in your court if your loyalty matches. Playing a patriot of a different coalition forces a loyalty change.
- **Prize**: Some cards have a prize icon. Prizes can be claimed through the Betray action.
- **Leveraged icon**: When played, take 2 rupees from the bank. When this card is discarded, you must return 2 rupees to the supply. For each rupee you cannot pay, discard 1 card from your hand or court.

---

## Loyalty and Influence

### Loyalty

Every player is always loyal to exactly one coalition: British, Russian, or Afghan. Your loyalty is set via your loyalty dial.

- **Starting loyalty**: During setup, each player chooses a starting loyalty (in clockwise order from a random start player).
- **Changing loyalty**: You change loyalty when you gain an influence point associated with a different coalition (by playing a patriot of another coalition or taking a prize of another coalition via Betray).
- **When loyalty changes**: Immediately discard all your patriots and prizes of your previous coalition, and return all gifts to your supply. Then adjust your loyalty dial.
- Coalition blocks you previously placed remain on the map but no longer count as "yours" for ruling purposes. Your cylinders always remain yours regardless of loyalty.

### Influence

Your total influence in your coalition equals: **1 (base) + number of patriots in your court + number of prizes + number of gifts**.

Influence determines scoring during successful Dominance Checks.

---

## Ruling a Region

A player rules a region if they have:
1. **At least one tribe** in that region, AND
2. A **plurality of ruling pieces** (more than any other single player individually).

**Ruling pieces** = your tribes + armies loyal to your coalition in that region.

- If there is a tie in ruling pieces, NO player rules the region.
- Enemy coalition armies (armies of coalitions you are not loyal to) can prevent you from ruling even if no enemy tribes are present.
- When you become ruler, take that region's ruler token. When you stop ruling, return it immediately.

### Benefits of ruling:
- Access to the **Build** action in that region.
- Ability to **tax** players with court cards in that region.
- Collect **bribes** from other players who play cards associated with that region.

## Setup

### Starting Favored Suit
Place the favored suit marker on **Political**.

### Build the Draw Deck
1. Separate court cards from event cards.
2. Shuffle court cards. Create **6 face-down piles**, each with **5 + N cards** (N = number of players). Remaining court cards are not used.
3. Separate the **4 Dominance Check** event cards. Place **one Dominance Check in each of the 4 rightmost piles** (piles 3–6).
4. Shuffle the remaining 12 event cards. Place **2 events in pile 2** and **1 event in each of piles 3–6**. The 6 remaining events are not used.
5. **Separately shuffle each pile**. Stack them so that the 4 piles with Dominance Checks are on the **bottom** of the deck. Do NOT shuffle the combined deck.

### Deck Size by Player Count
| Players | Cards per pile | Total court cards used |
|---------|---------------|----------------------|
| 2       | 7 per pile    | 42 court cards       |
| 3       | 8 per pile    | 48 court cards       |
| 4       | 9 per pile    | 54 court cards       |
| 5       | 10 per pile   | 60 court cards       |

### Create the Market
The market is **2 rows x 6 columns = 12 face-up cards**. Fill each column (top row first), starting from the leftmost column. Place the draw deck to the right of the market.

### Player Setup
Each player receives:
- 11 cylinders (1 on VP track at 0, 10 on player board)
- 1 loyalty dial
- 1 player board
- 4 rupees

### Starting Loyalty
Starting with a random player and proceeding clockwise, each player chooses a starting loyalty. The **last player to choose** takes the **first turn**. Play proceeds clockwise.

---

## Sequence of Play

Each turn consists of:
1. **Take up to 2 actions** (Purchase, Play, or any card-based action). You may take fewer or zero actions.
2. **Bonus actions** from cards matching the favored suit do NOT count against the 2-action limit. Each card can still only be used for one action once per turn.
3. **Cleanup** (performed after actions).

### Cleanup (4 steps)

1. **Court limit**: If you have more court cards than **3 + sum of purple stars** in your court, discard court cards down to that limit.
2. **Hand limit**: If you have more hand cards than **2 + sum of blue stars** in your court, discard hand cards down to that limit.
3. **Discard leftmost market events**: Discard any event cards in the **leftmost column** of the market. Top row is discarded first, then bottom row. When an event is discarded, its **top effect** applies to all players. Rupees on discarded events remain in their position on the market.
4. **Refill the market**: Slide all cards in each row (with their rupees) to the leftmost positions. Draw new cards from the deck to fill empty rightmost spaces. If a card slides into a position with rupees (from a previously discarded event), those rupees are placed on the card.

### Instability
If a Dominance Check card is revealed during market refill and there is **already a Dominance Check in the market**, immediately perform a Dominance Check, then discard both Dominance Check cards and refill. If the final Dominance Check was involved, it counts as the final check.

---

## Core Actions

### Purchase

Purchase a card from the market and add it to your hand.

**Cost**: Determined by the card's column position. The leftmost column is **free (0 rupees)**. Each subsequent column costs **1 more rupee** (0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5). Pay the cost by placing **one rupee on each card in the same row** to the left of the purchased card.

- If a market slot to the left is vacant, pay the rupee to the card in the **same column in the other row**.
- **Critical rule**: If you place a rupee on a market card for any reason, you may NOT purchase that card this turn.
- Any rupees already on the purchased card go to the purchasing player along with the card.

**Event cards**: Never enter your hand. Resolved immediately when purchased. Dominance Check events trigger a Dominance Check. Persistent events are placed below your court or near the map.

**Military favored**: When military is the favored suit, purchase costs are **doubled** — place **2 rupees** on each card to the left instead of 1.

### Play

Play a card from your hand to your court (either the left or right end — your choice).

**Bribes**: If another player rules the card's region, you must pay them a bribe equal to the **number of their tribes** in that region. The ruler may waive part or all of the bribe. If you decline to pay, the action is cancelled (you do not lose the action).

**Patriots**: If the played card is a patriot that does NOT match your current loyalty, discard all your current patriots and prizes, remove all gifts, and change your loyalty dial to match the patriot's coalition.

**Impact icons**: After placing the card, resolve each impact icon from top to bottom:
- **Army**: Place 1 coalition block of your loyalty in the card's region.
- **Road**: Place 1 coalition block of your loyalty on any border adjacent to the card's region.
- **Tribe**: Place 1 of your cylinders in the card's region.
- **Spy**: Place 1 of your cylinders on any court card (any player's court) that matches the played card's region.
- **Leverage**: Take 2 rupees from the bank. (Remember: if this card is later discarded, you must repay.)
- **Favored suit change**: Move the favored suit marker to the suit indicated on the card.

---

## Card-Based Actions

These actions require a card in your court that displays the corresponding action icon. Each card can only be used for **one action once per turn**, even if it has multiple action icons.

### Bonus Actions

Actions taken on cards that match the **favored suit** are bonus actions and do NOT count against your 2-action limit. Each card can still only be used once per turn.

### Action Costs

Some card-based actions cost rupees. These are paid by placing rupees on the **rightmost market cards** in both rows (1 rupee per card). If a market slot is vacant, skip it. If you place a rupee on a market card, you cannot purchase it this turn. If the market doesn't have enough cards, excess rupees are removed from the game.

### Hostage Actions

A card in your court is **held hostage** if a single enemy player has **more spies** on it than you (and more than any other individual player). To use a hostage card's actions, you must pay a bribe to the hostage-holder equal to the number of their spies on the card. The hostage-holder may waive part or all of this bribe.

**Special abilities (text box abilities) are NEVER held hostage.**

---

### Tax

Take rupees **up to the acting card's rank** from:
- **Players** who have at least one court card associated with a region you rule, OR
- **Market cards** (regardless of region).

You may split the total across multiple sources as long as the total does not exceed the card's rank.

**Tax shelter**: A player's total gold/economic stars in their court = their tax shelter. Only rupees **in excess** of this shelter can be taxed. Example: a player with 2 gold stars and 5 rupees can only have 3 rupees taxed.

### Gift

Place one of your cylinders on an **empty gift space** on your loyalty dial. Each gift costs the marked price: **2, 4, or 6 rupees** (paid to the market as action costs).

Each gift counts as **1 influence point** in your current coalition.

**Gifts are lost (returned to supply) whenever you change loyalty.**

### Build

Place **up to 3 armies and/or roads** among regions that you **rule**. Roads may be placed on any border adjacent to a ruled region. Any combination is allowed. Cost: **2 rupees per unit placed** (paid to the market as action costs).

You must rule at least one region to use this action.

### Move

Move loyal armies and/or spies. The number of moves equals the **acting card's rank** (1–3 moves).

- The same unit can be moved multiple times.
- Moves can be split across different armies and spies.

**Army movement**: An army moves from one region to an **adjacent region**. There must be a **road of the same coalition** as the army on the border being crossed. (Some special abilities override this road requirement.)

**Spy movement**: Spies move along court cards as if all players' courts formed a **single continuous circular track** around the table. Spies move clockwise or counter-clockwise, one card per move.

### Betray

Discard **one card** (from any player's court, including your own) where you have at least one **spy**. All spies on the betrayed card are returned to their owners' supplies. Cost: **2 rupees** (paid to the market).

After discarding the card, you **may** accept it as a **prize**, tucking it behind your loyalty dial. If the prize's coalition differs from your current loyalty, discard all your current patriots, prizes, and gifts, then change loyalty to match the prize.

**Betrayals may trigger**:
- **Leveraged penalties** (the card's owner must repay 2 rupees or discard cards)
- **The Overthrow Rule** (see below)

### Battle

Start a battle in a **single region** or on a **single court card**. Remove any combination of tribes, spies, roads, or armies at that location, up to the **acting card's rank**.

**Three restrictions**:
1. You cannot remove more units than the number of **your armies or spies** at that battle site.
2. You cannot remove **armies or roads of your own coalition**.
3. You cannot remove **tribes belonging to players who share your loyalty**. However, you CAN remove their spies.

**Battles may trigger the Overthrow Rule.**

---

## The Overthrow Rule

This is a critical rule that connects court cards to the map:

- If you lose your **last tribe in a region**, you must immediately **discard all political cards** of that region from your court.
- If you lose your **last political card** of a region from your court, you must immediately **remove all your tribes** in that region (returned to supply).

This creates a cascading vulnerability: losing tribes can cost you political cards, and losing political cards can cost you tribes.

---

## General Rules

### Negotiation
Players may freely discuss and coordinate actions. All deals are **non-binding**. Cards may never be transferred between players. Money can only change hands through game-sanctioned means (taxation, bribes, etc.).

### Component Limits
If you must place a piece and none remain in your supply, you must take a piece of the required type from **anywhere in play** (excluding pieces placed this turn). You may convert one unit type to another (e.g., take a tribe and place it as a spy).

### Card Precedence
Event cards and special abilities on court cards **override** standard rules. Special abilities are active as long as the card is in your court.

### Discarding a Court Card
Whenever a court card is discarded (for any reason):
- All spies on it are returned to their owners' supplies.
- If the card was **leveraged**, the owner must return 2 rupees to the supply. For each rupee they cannot pay, they must discard 1 card from their hand or court.

## Dominance Checks

### Triggering

Dominance Checks are triggered when:
1. A player **purchases** a Dominance Check event card from the market.
2. A Dominance Check card is **discarded during cleanup** (falls off the left end of the market).
3. **Instability**: A second Dominance Check card appears in the market while one is already there.

### Determining Dominance

Count all coalition blocks (armies + roads) on the map for each coalition. A coalition is **dominant** if it has **at least 4 more blocks** than every other coalition individually (not combined).

Example: British has 8 blocks, Russian has 4, Afghan has 4. British is dominant (8 - 4 = 4, meets the threshold).

Example: British has 7, Russian has 4, Afghan has 3. British is NOT dominant vs Russian (7 - 4 = 3, below threshold).

### Successful Check (A Coalition Dominates)

Players loyal to the dominant coalition score based on **influence**:
- **1st most influence**: 5 victory points
- **2nd most influence**: 3 victory points
- **3rd most influence**: 1 victory point

**Tie-breaking for influence scores**: Add up the VP for tied positions and divide by the number of tied players (round down). Example: Two players tied for 1st each score (5+3) / 2 = 4 VP.

**After scoring**: Remove **ALL coalition blocks** from the map (all coalitions, not just the dominant one). This represents an uneasy peace.

### Unsuccessful Check (No Coalition Dominates)

All players score based on the number of **cylinders in play** (any cylinder not on their player board — tribes, spies, and gifts all count):
- **1st most cylinders**: 3 victory points
- **2nd most cylinders**: 1 victory point

Tie-breaking works the same way (add tied positions' VP, divide by number tied, round down). Example: Two players tied for 1st each score (3+1) / 2 = 2 VP.

### The Final Dominance Check

All points earned during the **final (4th) Dominance Check are doubled**. Doubling occurs before splitting for ties.

### Early End

After any Dominance Check, if a single player leads all others by **at least 4 victory points**, the game ends immediately and that player wins.

---

## Game End and Victory

The game ends when:
1. A player achieves a **4+ VP lead** after any Dominance Check, OR
2. The **final (4th) Dominance Check** is resolved.

The player with the **most victory points** wins.

### Tiebreakers (in order)
1. Most **red/military stars** in court among tied players.
2. Most **rupees** among tied players.

---

## Favored Suit Details

The favored suit affects gameplay in several ways:
- **Bonus actions**: Card-based actions on cards matching the favored suit do NOT count against your 2-action limit.
- **Military favored**: When military is favored, **purchase costs are doubled** (2 rupees per card to the left instead of 1).
- **Suit changes**: When a card with a favored-suit-change impact icon is played, the favored suit changes to the indicated suit.

The game starts with **Political** as the favored suit.

---

## Privileges of Rank (Suit Star Totals)

Your privileges are determined by the sum of stars of each suit across all cards in your court:

| Suit | Privilege | Base Value |
|------|-----------|------------|
| Political (purple) | Court size limit | 3 + purple stars |
| Intelligence (blue) | Hand size limit | 2 + blue stars |
| Economic (gold) | Tax shelter | 0 + gold stars (rupees protected from tax) |
| Military (red) | Final tiebreaker | Sum of red stars |

## Player Count Adjustments

### 2 Players
Standard rules apply. More tactical, less diplomatic. Wakhan (solo AI) can be added as a third player.

### 3 Players
Standard rules. Good balance of diplomacy and strategy.

### 4–5 Players
Standard rules. Full diplomacy. Dominance is harder to achieve, making unsuccessful checks (cylinder-based scoring) more common.

### 1 Player (Solo with Wakhan)
Uses the Wakhan automated opponent. See Wakhan section below.

## Wakhan (Solo / 2-Player Automated Opponent)

Wakhan is an automated opponent representing a radical ideology that transcends traditional loyalties. Wakhan can be faced by 1 or 2 human players. Only one player (or Wakhan) can win — it is NOT cooperative.

### Wakhan Setup

- Include Wakhan as a player when determining deck size.
- Shuffle the 24 AI cards and place them face down as a draw deck.
- Wakhan uses a spare set of player cylinders but does NOT take a loyalty dial (uses her aid card for gifts).
- Place Wakhan's pieces to the right of the last player who chose loyalty. **Wakhan takes the first turn**.

### Wakhan General Rules

- Wakhan pays all costs including bribes like a regular player.
- If a special ability says Wakhan "may" do something, she always will.
- **Wakhan is loyal to ALL coalitions simultaneously**. She can hold patriots and prizes of different coalitions. She scores during any successful Dominance Check regardless of which coalition dominates.
- **Pragmatic Loyalty**: Wakhan's active loyalty (for placing blocks, moving, battling) is the **leftmost loyalty on the current AI card** that is NOT shared by any other player.
- For ruling regions, count Wakhan's tribes plus only the **most numerous armies of a single coalition** she has in that region.
- If Wakhan must choose a suit, she always chooses the current favored suit.
- If Wakhan must discard a leveraged card and has no coins, she does not need to discard additional cards.

### Wakhan's Turn

1. Draw an AI card, place it face up in a discard pile.
2. Use the drawn card (face) and the **back of the next card** on the draw deck to make decisions.
3. If the draw deck is empty, reshuffle the entire discard pile (including the card just drawn) and draw again.
4. Perform **2 actions** by reading the AI card's action section from top to bottom. If she finishes the bottom action and still has actions remaining, start again from the top.
5. Bonus actions: After 2 regular actions, take any available bonus actions from favored-suit court cards (leftmost card first, leftmost action on each card).

### Card Priority (for choosing cards)

From highest to lowest priority:
1. Opponent's card (not Wakhan's own)
2. Matches favored suit
3. Patriot of the dominant coalition
4. Has a prize matching the dominant coalition
5. Other patriot
6. Leveraged
7. Highest ranking
8. Highest card number (final tiebreaker)

### Discard Priority (for cleanup)

From highest to lowest discard priority:
1. Non-political cards
2. Non-patriots
3. Non-leveraged
4. Cards with most player spies exceeding Wakhan's spies
5. Fewest total spies
6. Lowest rank
7. Not matching favored suit
8. Lowest card number

### Wakhan's Core Action: Radicalize

Wakhan does not use Purchase/Play separately. Instead she **Radicalizes** — purchasing one card from the market and immediately playing it (counts as 1 action).

**When radicalizing**:
- **With specific AI card instructions**: Follow them. Ties broken by cheapest card, then highest card number.
- **Dominance Check in market**: Wakhan only purchases a DC if she would score the most points/win. Otherwise: buy cheapest patriot of dominant coalition, then cheapest card with most army/road icons. If no coalition dominant, cheapest card with most spy/tribe icons.
- **Otherwise**: Use the red arrow (Top/Bottom row) and black arrow (column 0–5) on the back of the draw deck's top card. If that card isn't valid, pick next valid card to its left; if row exhausted, switch to original position in other row.

**After purchasing**: Wakhan plays the card if she can afford the bribe. If she cannot, she discards it. Red arrow pointing to "top" = play to left side of court; "bottom" = play to right side.

**Impact icon modifications**:
- **Spies**: Place on highest priority cards of the played card's region where Wakhan doesn't have the most spies.
- **Roads**: Place on consecutive borders following region priority on the AI card. If roads remain after one pass, go through priority again.
- **Patriots**: Always place blocks based on pragmatic loyalty (ignore patriot icon colors for armies/roads).

### Wakhan's Card-Based Actions

- **Gift**: Buy cheapest gift she can afford. Gifts count as influence in ALL three coalitions.
- **Build**: Build armies in the leftmost region (AI card priority) that she rules. Spend as much money as possible.
- **Betray**: Betray highest priority card with a loyalty prize where she has a spy. Always takes the prize.
- **Battle**: Battle in region where another player has pieces and Wakhan has at least one army. Destruction priority: tribes > armies > roads. If no valid region, battle on highest priority court card where she and another player have spies. Red arrow resolves ties between players.
- **Tax**: Tax players before market cards. Tax players with most rupees first. Red arrow breaks ties.
- **Move**: Move only armies (no road required). Move to adjacent regions where other players have tribes. Seek to match army count to tribe count. Will not move if it would lose a ruler token. AI card region priority breaks ties.

### Wakhan's Ambition

If a Dominance Check is in the market and Wakhan can purchase it AND would score the most VP (and/or win), she will purchase it regardless of her AI card instructions.

### Wakhan's Supply

When Wakhan is out of pieces, she takes from elsewhere in play. Priority: tribes > armies > roads > spies. Use AI card for location decisions (except spies, which use card priority order).

### Wakhan Dominance and Victory

Wakhan scores VP like a regular player. Since she is loyal to all coalitions, she is always in contention during successful checks. Standard early-end and final scoring rules apply.

## Commonly Misunderstood Rules and Edge Cases

### Loyalty
- **Loyalty changes are triggered by gaining influence in another coalition** (playing a different patriot or taking a different-coalition prize via Betray). Simply purchasing a card does not change loyalty.
- When you change loyalty, you lose ALL gifts, prizes, and patriots of your previous coalition. This is a significant cost.
- You always have a loyalty (chosen at setup). You cannot be "neutral."

### Purchasing
- You CANNOT purchase a card that you placed a rupee on this turn (even if paying for a different purchase or action cost).
- Rupees on purchased cards go to the buyer.
- Event cards are resolved immediately upon purchase — they never enter your hand.
- When military is favored, purchase costs double (2 rupees per card to the left).

### Playing Cards
- Cards are placed at either the LEFT or RIGHT end of your court — your choice. Courts cannot be rearranged.
- Bribes to rulers are based on the ruler's number of tribes, NOT ruling pieces.
- If you decline to pay a bribe, you keep your action (it is not lost).

### Actions
- You get UP TO 2 actions per turn (you may take fewer).
- Bonus actions (favored suit cards) are unlimited in number but each card still only provides one action per turn.
- Each card can only be used for ONE of its actions ONCE per turn, even if it has multiple action icons.

### Battle
- You can remove spies belonging to players who share your loyalty, but NOT their tribes.
- You cannot remove armies or roads of your own coalition.
- The number of units you can remove is limited by BOTH the acting card's rank AND the number of your own armies/spies at the battle site (whichever is less).

### Betray
- You can betray cards in your OWN court (if you have a spy on them).
- Taking a prize is optional.
- Taking a prize of a different coalition triggers a full loyalty change (losing all previous patriots, prizes, gifts).

### Tax
- You can tax players OR market cards (or a combination), up to the acting card's rank total.
- Taxing players requires you to RULE a region where they have a court card.
- Tax shelter protects a number of rupees equal to the player's gold/economic stars.

### Dominance Checks
- The threshold is always 4 more blocks than each other coalition INDIVIDUALLY (not combined).
- During unsuccessful checks, cylinders "in play" means ALL cylinders not on your player board — tribes, spies, AND gifts all count.
- The final Dominance Check doubles points BEFORE splitting for ties.
- After a successful check, ALL coalition blocks are removed from the map (not just the dominant coalition's).

### The Overthrow Rule
- Losing your last tribe in a region forces you to discard ALL political cards of that region.
- Losing your last political card of a region forces removal of ALL your tribes in that region.
- These can cascade: losing a political card to overthrow can trigger tribe removal, which can trigger more political card losses.

### Component Limits
- If you must place a piece and have none in your supply, take one from anywhere in play (not placed this turn) and convert it.

### Court and Hand Limits
- Court limit = 3 + purple stars. Hand limit = 2 + blue stars.
- Limits are only enforced during cleanup, not during your turn. Your court/hand can temporarily exceed limits.
- You choose which cards to discard when over the limit.

### Market
- The market is 2 rows x 6 columns (12 cards).
- Cards slide LEFT when gaps appear. New cards fill from the RIGHT.
- Rupees on events that are discarded remain in their market position and transfer to whatever card slides into that spot.

### Spies
- Spies can be placed on ANY player's court card (including your own) that matches the relevant region.
- Spies move through courts as if they form a circular track around the table.
- Spies enable Betray actions and contribute to holding cards hostage.
- Spies count as "cylinders in play" for unsuccessful Dominance Checks.

### Hostage
- A card is held hostage when a SINGLE enemy player has MORE spies on it than any other individual player (including the card's owner).
- The bribe equals the number of hostage-holding spies.
- Special abilities are NEVER held hostage — only card-based actions.

### Map
- There is no limit on pieces per region or border.
- Pieces from different players and coalitions can coexist in the same location.
- The 6 regions and their border connections define army movement paths.

## Map Geography

### Regions and Borders

The 6 regions are connected as follows:
- **Transcaspia** borders: Herat, Kabul, Persia
- **Persia** borders: Transcaspia, Herat
- **Herat** borders: Transcaspia, Persia, Kabul, Kandahar
- **Kabul** borders: Transcaspia, Herat, Kandahar, Punjab
- **Kandahar** borders: Herat, Kabul, Punjab
- **Punjab** borders: Kabul, Kandahar

## Quick Reference: Turn Structure

1. **Actions (up to 2)**:
   - **Purchase**: Buy a market card (cost = column position, pay rupees to left cards)
   - **Play**: Play a hand card to court (pay bribe if another player rules the region)
   - **Card action**: Use one action from a court card (Tax, Gift, Build, Move, Betray, or Battle)
   - **Bonus**: Card actions on favored-suit cards are free (don't count toward 2-action limit)

2. **Cleanup**:
   - Discard court cards exceeding court limit (3 + purple stars)
   - Discard hand cards exceeding hand limit (2 + blue stars)
   - Discard leftmost market events (resolve top effects)
   - Refill market (slide left, draw right)

## Quick Reference: Dominance Check Scoring

| Check Result | 1st Place | 2nd Place | 3rd Place |
|-------------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Successful (coalition dominant) | 5 VP | 3 VP | 1 VP |
| Unsuccessful (no dominance) | 3 VP | 1 VP | — |
| Final check | All points doubled | All points doubled | All points doubled |

**Ties**: Add VP for tied positions, divide by number of tied players, round down.

**Early end**: If any player leads by 4+ VP after a check, game ends immediately.
