# Arcs: Collapse and Conflict in the Reach — Rules Reference

## Overview

Arcs is an asymmetric strategy game by Cole Wehrle (Leder Games) for 2–4 players. Players lead rival factions vying for power in a dying interstellar empire. The game is played over a series of chapters (rounds), with each chapter consisting of several acts. It features a unique trick-taking action system where the lead player sets the suit (action type) and other players must follow or pivot.

---

## Components

- 1 Map board with a cluster of star systems connected by paths
- Court cards (political figures, guilds, relics)
- Action cards in 4 suits (with values 1–7)
- Faction pieces: ships, agents, cities, starports, lore tokens
- Resource tokens: fuel, material, relic, weapon, psionic
- Ambition markers (scoring objectives)
- Chapter and act tracker

---

## Core Concepts

### The Map
The map consists of star systems (called "systems") connected by gates (paths). Each system has a number of building slots and can hold ships, agents, buildings (cities and starports), and resource tokens.

### Pieces
- **Ships**: Your mobile military units. Used for battles and controlling systems.
- **Agents**: Placed in the court. Represent political influence.
- **Cities**: Buildings that generate resources and provide control.
- **Starports**: Buildings that provide tactical advantages and trade.

### Resources
- **Fuel**: Used for powering actions and sustaining fleets.
- **Material**: Used for building.
- **Relics**: Valuable items for scoring.
- **Weapons**: Military supplies.
- **Psionic**: Rare mystical resources.

### Control
You **control** a system if you have more loyal pieces (ships + buildings) there than any single rival. Control matters for collecting resources, building, and scoring.

---

## Game Structure

### Chapters
A game of Arcs is played over a set number of chapters (typically 3–5). At the end of each chapter, ambitions are scored.

### Acts
Each chapter consists of a series of acts. In each act, one player is the **lead** and the others must follow.

### Initiative
The player with the **initiative marker** leads the first act of each chapter. Initiative passes to the player who "seized" it during the chapter (by playing a specific card combination), or defaults to the player clockwise from the current holder.

---

## The Action System (Trick-Taking)

This is Arcs' signature mechanic. Actions are driven by playing cards in a trick-taking format.

### Suits (Action Types)
Each card belongs to one of four suits, and each suit corresponds to an action type:
1. **Mobilize** (move ships)
2. **Administer** (build, tax, repair)
3. **Aggress** (battle, raid, abduct)
4. **Influence** (manipulate the court, secure agents)

### Leading
The lead player plays a card from their hand, setting the suit for the act. The lead's card value determines the number of pips (action points) they get.

### Following
Each subsequent player must either:
1. **Follow suit**: Play a card of the same suit. They get pips equal to their card value.
2. **Pivot**: Play a card of a different suit. They only get 1 pip, regardless of value. However, pivoting gives flexibility.
3. **Surpass**: Play a card of the same suit with a higher value than the lead. You become the new lead (take the initiative) and get your full pips. The previous lead keeps their pips but loses initiative.
4. **Copy**: Some abilities let you copy the lead's suit.

### Pips
Pips are your action points for the act. Each pip lets you perform one unit of the act's suit action:
- **Mobilize pip**: Move 1 ship to an adjacent system.
- **Administer pip**: Build 1 building, tax 1 system, or repair 1 piece.
- **Aggress pip**: Initiate or add to a battle, raid, or abduct.
- **Influence pip**: Place or move 1 agent in the court, or secure 1 card.

---

## Actions in Detail

### Mobilize (Move)
Each pip moves 1 ship along a gate to an adjacent system. Fresh ships (not damaged) can move. Ships can move through systems containing rivals but may provoke interception.

### Administer
Each pip lets you choose one:
- **Build**: Place a city or starport in a system you control with an empty building slot. Costs material resources.
- **Tax**: Collect a resource from a system you control.
- **Repair**: Flip a damaged piece back to fresh.

### Aggress
Each pip lets you choose one:
- **Battle**: Assign attacking ships. Battles are resolved with dice — each attacking ship rolls and hits on certain results. Defenders can also roll.
- **Raid**: Steal a resource from a rival's supply in a system where you have ships.
- **Abduct**: Remove a rival's agent from the court (requires presence in the same court area).

### Influence
Each pip lets you choose one:
- **Place agent**: Add an agent to a court card.
- **Move agent**: Move an agent between court cards.
- **Secure**: Take a court card you have influence over into your control (costs agents).

---

## Battle Resolution

When you initiate battle:
1. **Declare** the system and your attacking ships.
2. The defender chooses which pieces to defend with.
3. **Roll dice**: The attacker rolls dice equal to their fresh ships (up to a cap). Each die can produce a hit, a self-damage, or a miss.
4. **Assign hits**: Hits damage defender's pieces (ships first, then buildings). Damaged pieces are flipped; already-damaged pieces are destroyed.
5. **Defender retaliates**: If the defender has fresh ships remaining, they roll and assign hits to the attacker.
6. After battle, check control of the system.

### Interception
When ships move into or through a system with rival ships, the rival may **intercept** — forcing a battle in that system before the moving ships continue.

---

## The Court

The court is a row of face-up cards representing political actors, guilds, and resources. Players compete for influence over court cards by placing agents on them.

- Each court card provides a benefit (ongoing ability, resources, scoring bonus).
- You **influence** a card if you have the most agents on it.
- You can **secure** a card (take it for yourself) by spending agents from it.
- Some court cards provide powerful one-time effects when secured.

---

## Ambitions (Scoring)

Ambitions are the scoring objectives for each chapter. At the start of a chapter, ambition markers are placed, defining what scores points.

### Ambition Types
- **Warlord**: Score for controlling systems and having the most military presence.
- **Keeper**: Score for holding the most resources and relics.
- **Empath**: Score for having the most influence in the court.
- **Tyrant**: Score for buildings and economic dominance.
- **Tycoon**: Score for having the most material wealth.

Players declare ambitions during the chapter by placing their markers. Each ambition scores at end of chapter — first place gets more points than second.

### Power vs. Trophies
- **Power**: Your long-term score. You want to accumulate power.
- **Trophies**: End-of-chapter bonuses. Trophies from ambitions convert to power.

---

## Fate and Lore

### Lore Tokens
Placed by certain actions or events. Lore tokens on systems can trigger narrative events and provide scoring opportunities.

### Fate Cards
Special event cards that introduce dramatic twists. They can change the rules temporarily, create crises, or offer opportunities.

---

## Campaign Mode

Arcs has a **campaign mode** where chapters carry over, creating an evolving narrative. Between chapters:
- Players keep some resources and influence.
- The political landscape shifts based on previous results.
- New factions and powers may emerge.
- Lore tokens accumulate, building toward climactic events.

---

## Key Timing Rules

- **Initiative**: The player who surpassed last holds initiative for the next act.
- **Interception**: Happens immediately when ships enter a system with rivals.
- **Court effects**: Trigger when specified (on secure, on influence, ongoing).
- **Ambition declaration**: Can only be done during specific windows.
- **Resource limits**: Players have supply limits; excess resources are discarded.

---

## Setup (3-4 Player Game)

1. Place the map board.
2. Shuffle the action deck and deal starting hands.
3. Set up the court with a number of face-up court cards.
4. Each player places their starting pieces on the map according to faction setup.
5. Place resource tokens on systems as indicated.
6. Set ambition markers on the chapter track.
7. Give the initiative marker to the appropriate starting player.
8. Begin the first chapter.

## FAQ / Common Misunderstandings

**Q: Can I play any suit if I don't have the lead suit?**
A: Yes, but you "pivot" and only get 1 pip regardless of card value.

**Q: What happens if I surpass the lead?**
A: You take initiative (become the new lead) and use your card's full pips. The previous lead keeps their pips.

**Q: Can ships move through enemy systems?**
A: Yes, but the enemy may intercept, forcing a battle.

**Q: How does control work?**
A: You control a system if your loyal pieces (ships + buildings) outnumber any single rival's pieces there. If tied, no one controls it.

**Q: When do ambitions score?**
A: At the end of each chapter. All declared ambitions score simultaneously.

**Q: Can I battle in a system without ships?**
A: No. You need ships to attack. Buildings alone cannot initiate battles.
